Vendor details

Pinecone Quiet

Nothing from Pinecone between 21 and 24 September; the 2026 changelog has no entry inside this window.

Standing items: full-text search and the Documents API reached general availability on API version 2026-07 on 2 September, combining BM25 keyword ranking, dense-vector and sparse-vector ranking in schema-based document indexes queryable by BM25 token matching, Lucene query syntax or vector similarity. Read-egress metering began 1 September, with monthly allowances of 1GB on Starter, 10GB on Builder and 100GB on Standard and Enterprise, and the one-time $250 bulk-import credit for new Standard and Enterprise subscriptions ended the same day, with remaining balances expiring 1 November 2026. Bring Your Own Cloud reached general availability on 25 August across AWS, GCP and Azure; Pinecone Nexus reached general availability on 6 August; Pinecone Marketplace reached end of life on 31 August. The last disclosed funding round remains a $100 million Series B from April 2023.

Weaviate Quiet

Nothing from Weaviate between 21 and 24 September.

Standing items: on 17 September Weaviate published a detailed piece on 4-bit rotational quantisation in Weaviate 1.39, covering SIMD performance work and a TurboQuant comparison; on 9 September, an engineering post on HFresh, its disk-based vector index for memory-efficient vector search; on 1 September, late-interaction multi-vector retrieval enabling PDF search by page appearance without OCR or chunking. Weaviate 1.39 shipped 27 August, promoting the Boost API and MMR diversity selection to general availability and previewing an experimental Search REST API. Ricoh invested in Weaviate through the RICOH Innovation Fund in a round announced 16 June; the last disclosed priced round remains a $50 million Series B in 2024.

OpenSearch (AWS / Linux Foundation) Active

OpenSearchCon North America is running 22–24 September at the San Jose Marriott, organised by the Linux Foundation to mark the OpenSearch Project's fifth anniversary. The keynote comes from Tia White, general manager of OpenSearch at AWS, who leads the teams behind AWS's search, analytics, vector database and observability services; the agenda pairs more than 60 keynotes, lightning talks, breakout sessions and hands-on workshops with case studies from Booking.com, Groupon, LinkedIn and Uber on architecting agentic AI at enterprise scale. Live attendees receive a $250 voucher toward the OpenSearch Certified Associate certification course.

Alongside the conference, OpenSearch maintainers opened the coordinated release-engineering process for OpenSearch 3.10.0 on 22 September: a manifest was created at manifests/3.10.0 in the opensearch-build repository, and matching "Release version 3.10.0" tracking issues were opened across more than a dozen component repositories, including skills, notifications, index-management, search-relevance, cross-cluster-replication, opensearch-learning-to-rank-base, job-scheduler and common-utils. This is release-engineering activity, not a confirmed shipped build; no GA announcement for 3.10.0 had been published at this edition's timestamp.

Watch signal: A fifth-anniversary conference and a coordinated 3.10 release cycle landing in the same 72 hours is unlikely to be coincidental. Expect AWS and the OpenSearch Project to use the conference to narrate a full year of agentic-AI and vector-search progress, which is the framing Elastic field teams should be ready to counter.

Standing items: OpenSearch 3.8.0, shipped 5 August, remains the latest confirmed stable release, adding experimental retention policies for agentic memory that automatically expire sessions, long-term memories and history entries. Amazon OpenSearch Service extended automatic semantic enrichment to VPC-enabled domains in August, and added OpenSearch 3.7 support with 1-bit scalar quantisation on both the Faiss and Lucene engines, plus an Agent Toolkit for AWS integration that drives domains and Serverless collections from Claude Code, Kiro and Cursor, in July.

Algolia Quiet

Nothing new from Algolia between 21 and 24 September.

Standing items: the 9 September appointment of Steven Chung as chief revenue officer, from Starburst, and Veronica Servantez as chief marketing officer, from Genesys, remains the most recent leadership news. DevCon 2026, a free virtual developer conference built around AI agents, MCP-integrated coding agents and Agent Studio, is confirmed for 1 October. Algolia was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Search and Product Discovery in June, its third consecutive year, and took 12 badges in the G2 Spring 2026 Grid reports in April.

Qdrant Quiet

Nothing new from Qdrant between 21 and 24 September; the blog's most recent post remains 16 September.

Standing items: the 16 September post is a case study on Bayer's myGenAssist running on Qdrant Hybrid Cloud at 135 million points, covering hybrid search for agents, semantic caching and multitenancy. On 1 September, Qdrant released Qdrant-FineWeb-10B, a 10-billion-vector open benchmark built with Vultr on the FineWeb corpus, alongside Supernova, an open-source load-testing framework that also targets Milvus and Elasticsearch. Qdrant 1.19 shipped 5 August with the TurboQuant datatype and memory tiers. The company's last disclosed round was a $50 million Series B in March 2026, led by Advance Venture Partners.

MongoDB (Atlas Search / Atlas Vector Search) Quiet

Nothing new from MongoDB Atlas Search or Atlas Vector Search between 21 and 24 September.

Standing items: on 10 September Atlas became a connected data source in OpenAI's Data Plugin via the Atlas Managed MCP Server, letting the ChatGPT Work Data agent query Atlas clusters directly and turn answers into reports or dashboards. On 9 September, Europe and US data residency for the Embedding and Reranking API Service entered public preview, giving European customers a local endpoint for Voyage AI models. MongoDB reported Q2 FY2027 revenue of $771.8 million on 1 September, up 30% year over year with Atlas up about 29%, and 70,600 customers after a record 2,900 net additions.

Databricks Quiet

Nothing search- or retrieval-relevant landed from Databricks between 21 and 24 September; the one dated platform item in the window, general availability of the system.query.history system table on 21 September, is an account-admin feature rather than a search capability.

Standing items: the generate_citations option in the ai_search function entered beta on 8 September, returning which retrieved chunks support a generated answer. External secrets in Unity Catalog entered beta on 9 September, alongside beta connectors for Celigo, Anysphere Organization and Anaplan. The Databricks Excel add-in reached general availability on 10 September. AI Search gained struct and map column indexing and filter-only queries on 2 September.

Vespa (Vespa.ai) Quiet

Nothing new from Vespa between 21 and 24 September.

Standing items: the September newsletter, published 8 September, added a ranking.matching.anntimebudget query parameter that caps ANN search by latency budget, per-label BM25 scoring via bm25(field, label) and a labeled() query operator documented as the equivalent of Elasticsearch's function_score, plus OpenTelemetry export for Vespa Cloud Enclave. Vespa.ai Live ran in London on 9–10 September with a keynote from chief executive Jon Bratseth and speakers from Walmart, Etsy and RavenPack. Vespa is scheduled to appear at Shoptalk Fall in Nashville from 29 September to 1 October.
