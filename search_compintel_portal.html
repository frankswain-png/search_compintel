TLDR

A second consecutive quiet window: none of the eight tracked vendors published a verifiable announcement between 5 and 8 September, and the board is now visibly holding announcements for a dense conference fortnight rather than shipping into early September. The most consequential standing item remains Qdrant's 1 September release of Qdrant-FineWeb-10B and the Supernova benchmarking framework, whose load-testing modules name Elasticsearch as a first-class target — and this run corrects the record on Qdrant's balance sheet: total funding is roughly $87.5 million after the $50 million Series B in March 2026, not $50 million. The item this tracker had been under-weighting is AWS's purpose-built log analytics engine for Amazon OpenSearch Service, announced 1 July 2026, which moves storage to Apache Parquet and claims 4x price-performance, 2x faster ingestion and up to 70% lower storage cost at no additional charge — a direct attack on the economics Elastic competes on, and the most likely subject of expansion announcements at OpenSearchCon. Pinecone, Weaviate, Algolia, MongoDB, Databricks and Vespa were otherwise silent. Six catalysts land inside three weeks, starting tomorrow: Vespa.ai Live in London on 9–10 September, MongoDB at three investor conferences on 9, 10 and 15 September, Qdrant's Vector Space Stream on 17 September, OpenSearchCon North America on 22–24 September, Algolia at AI Engineer Paris on 23–24 September, and Algolia DevCon opening 30 September.

Vendor detail

Pinecone

Quiet

Nothing verifiable from Pinecone in the 5–8 September window. A caution for anyone reading search results directly: the Ash Ashutosh CEO appointment and Edo Liberty's move to chief scientist are dated 8 September 2025, not 2026, and resurface every year on this date — the PR Newswire release ID places it firmly in the 2025 sequence. The genuine standing picture is Pinecone's repositioning from vector database to knowledge layer. Pinecone Nexus reached general availability on 6 August 2026, compiling enterprise data into governed, domain-specific knowledge artifacts that agents query through KnowQL, a declarative query language designed for agents rather than humans; Pinecone's own figures claim more than 90% lower token cost than agentic RAG, answers up to 30 times faster, and above 90% task accuracy, with a reported top score on τ-Knowledge, Sierra's open enterprise-knowledge benchmark. Nexus deploys into the customer's own cloud with zero vendor access, field-level access control, per-field citations, confidence scores, PII-aware ingestion and lineage back to source. Earlier 2026 groundwork included a Microsoft OneLake integration in June, Bring Your Own Cloud for data-residency customers, and Dedicated Read Nodes in public preview from December 2025. Ashutosh's first full year is the thing to watch: a CEO drawn from Google solution sales and three storage exits suggests enterprise go-to-market discipline rather than further category redefinition, which would make Pinecone a more conventional competitor in named accounts than it has been.

Weaviate

Quiet

No Weaviate announcements in the window, and no scheduled catalyst inside the next three weeks — the only tracked vendor with neither. The most recent release is v1.39.0 in August 2026, adding a new quantization method, new query surfaces, runtime control over schema, and performance work across search, replication and indexing; it follows the HFresh disk-based vector index of 25 June 2026, the pair addressing the memory-cost objection that has dogged Weaviate in large deployments. Free Weaviate Cloud access opened in June 2026 and Engram shipped in May 2026. On developer surface, Weaviate published Agent Skills as an open-source repository on 21 February 2026, equipping Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, VS Code and Gemini CLI with tools for generating production Weaviate code — a distribution play that puts Weaviate inside the coding agents where retrieval choices increasingly get made, and the most strategically interesting thing the company has done this year. On funding, reported total raised stands at roughly $67.7 million following the RICOH Innovation Fund investment completed 13 March 2026 and announced 16 June 2026 on undisclosed terms. That figure now sits below Qdrant's $87.5 million, which reverses the capitalisation order between the two open-source-first vector vendors and is worth tracking if Weaviate goes back to market.

OpenSearch (AWS / Linux Foundation)

Quiet

Nothing from the OpenSearch project or Amazon OpenSearch Service in the window, but the most competitively significant standing item on the whole board belongs here and predates this tracker's coverage of it. On 1 July 2026 AWS announced a purpose-built log analytics engine for Amazon OpenSearch Service that stores data in Apache Parquet and claims up to 4x price-performance, 2x faster ingestion, up to 2x faster analytical queries and up to 70% lower storage cost — delivered at no additional charge to existing customers. That is a columnar-storage repositioning of the managed service against exactly the log-volume economics Elastic sells on, and it is free rather than an upsell, which removes the usual friction from a migration conversation. The nearest catalyst is OpenSearchCon North America, 22–24 September at the San Jose Marriott, marking the project's fifth anniversary with 60-plus sessions across four tracks, a returning Unconference and a $250 certification voucher for live attendees; the Linux Foundation announcement of 23 July 2026 names Booking.com, Groupon, LinkedIn and Uber among speakers and frames the event around open-source search and observability powering agentic AI at enterprise scale. On product, OpenSearch 3.8.0 shipped 5 August 2026 with experimental retention policies for agentic memory, and Amazon OpenSearch Service added OpenSearch 3.7 support in July 2026 with 1-bit scalar quantization on both Faiss and Lucene engines plus an Agent Toolkit for AWS integration for driving domains from Claude Code, Kiro and Cursor. Expect September's conference to concentrate the announcements that did not trickle out this month, most plausibly extending the Parquet engine.

Algolia

Quiet

No Algolia news in the window; the company has two back-to-back September appearances and is evidently saving material for them. Algolia showcases Agent Studio at AI Engineer Paris on 23–24 September at Station F, then runs DevCon 2026 as a free virtual conference from 30 September to 1 October, whose published agenda is built around AI agents rather than site search — moving agent prototypes to production, MCP-integrated coding agents, and use cases on Agent Studio and NeuralSearch. Strategically, Algolia set out its position in "Six Bold Bets for 2026" on 13 January 2026, arguing that retrieval, scale and memory are the foundation of agentic AI and that generative discovery reinvents brand loyalty — a retrieval-infrastructure framing that moves Algolia onto the same ground Elastic occupies rather than the e-commerce search box where it has historically competed. The company reports 1.75 trillion searches a year for over 18,000 businesses, and was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Search and Product Discovery for the third consecutive year. Two dating traps to note: Agent Studio and the DocSearch/AskAI self-service launch both date to September 2025, not 2026, and resurface as current every autumn; and CTO Xavier Grand's AI Day France panel at Station F was 10 February 2026, not September.

Qdrant

Quiet

Nothing new from Qdrant inside the window, but the 1 September release of Qdrant-FineWeb-10B remains the most consequential competitive item of the past fortnight. Built with Vultr on Hugging Face's FineWeb corpus using Alibaba's gte-multilingual-base, the dataset carries 10.07 billion dense and 10.07 billion sparse vectors — 24.47 TB of vectors and 28.66 TB of source text and metadata — with exact brute-force top-1000 ground truth computed for 120,000 dense, sparse and filtered queries. Qdrant also released PubMed-Multi-Vector, holding the corpus constant across dense, sparse and ColBERT-style multi-vector representations at 8.37 billion multi-vector tokens, and Coyo-Vector-Embeddings for multimodal retrieval via Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B. The second-order read is Supernova, the open-source framework shipped alongside: its nova-load and nova-storm modules drive parallel ingestion and search stress tests against Qdrant, Milvus and Elasticsearch, tracking QPS, p50/p95/p99 latency, build time and recall against nova-bf ground truth. Qdrant has published a credible, reproducible harness in which Elasticsearch is a named target, framed as an attack on vendor benchmarks that use synthetic data, hidden queries and paywalled engines; press pickup followed on 1 September in Blocks & Files and 3 September in BigDATAwire. Two corrections to the standing record: Qdrant's total funding is roughly $87.5 million, not $50 million — the March 2026 Series B was $50 million, led by AVP with Bosch Ventures, Unusual Ventures, Spark Capital and 42CAP, on top of a $28 million Series A in 2024. And Vector Space Day in San Francisco has already happened: it ran on 11 June 2026 at The Midway with 350-plus attendees, and should not be described as upcoming. The live catalyst is Vector Space Stream on 17 September, a research-first broadcast of eight technical talks on compression, edge and hybrid search. Qdrant Edge, launched June 2026 as an in-process offline engine for robotics and edge AI, is in beta; Qdrant Serverless is listed as coming soon; GPU-accelerated indexing, multi-AZ clusters and audit logging landed in Qdrant Cloud in April 2026.

MongoDB (Atlas Search / Atlas Vector Search)

Quiet

No MongoDB product news in the window. The financial picture reported 1 September is the standing item: Q2 FY2027 revenue of $771.8 million, up 30% year over year and ahead of a roughly $734 million consensus, with Atlas up about 29% and EA and other up about 36%. MongoDB returned to GAAP profitability at $40.9 million net income, or $0.50 per share, against a $47 million loss a year earlier, and posted non-GAAP net income of $162.6 million, or $1.90 per diluted share, versus $1.00. It ended the quarter with 70,600 customers after a record 2,900 net additions, and raised full-year FY2027 guidance to $2.99–3.03 billion with non-GAAP EPS of $6.39–6.58. The 4 September investor-conference disclosure names three appearances rather than one, all still ahead: Citi 2026 Global TMT on 9 September at 8:50 a.m. ET, Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology on 10 September at 10:10 a.m. PT, and Piper Sandler Growth Frontiers on 15 September at 11:30 a.m. CT. Three appearances in seven days on the back of a beat-and-raise is a management team expecting questions about durability rather than one managing expectations downward, and the Atlas-versus-EA growth split — EA growing faster than Atlas — is the number worth listening for, because it points at self-managed and on-premises demand rather than cloud. On product, the relevant recent work is dated 13 August 2026: a Managed MCP Server connecting Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build and Devin directly to Atlas operational data, and an Atlas retrieval push comprising Automated Embeddings powered by Voyage AI, the Atlas Embedding and Reranking API, voyage-code-4, and vector search inside Atlas Stream Processing. Vector search over nested embeddings reached general availability on 30 June 2026. Note that the LangChain–MongoDB partnership currently resurfacing in search results dates from 31 March 2026, and the extension of search and vector search to Community Edition and Enterprise Server was announced at MongoDB.local NYC in September 2025 — neither is new.

Databricks

Quiet

Nothing dated 5–8 September on the Databricks release notes, which carry items through 4 September only. Those items are the standing picture, and one is directly competitive: on 2 September, AI Search added indexing and filtering on struct and map columns plus filter-only queries that return rows matching a filter with no vector or keyword retrieval at all — Databricks closing the gap on structured-filter retrieval patterns that have been a reason to keep a dedicated search engine in the stack. The renaming of Vector Search to AI Search is itself the signal: the product is being positioned to cover multiple retrieval patterns, not vector similarity alone, and it now runs HNSW ANN with L2 distance inside the governance and lineage perimeter of Unity Catalog, which is the part Elastic cannot match for a customer already standardised on the lakehouse. The rest is model and platform plumbing: OpenAI GPT-6 Astra on Unity Gateway for agentic search and long-context work on 4 September; OpenSharing for metric views in beta and environment version 6 on 3 September; Git Folder Serverless in beta and Google Gemini 3.8 Flash as a hosted model on 2 September; and on 1 September, Genie One web search in beta with source citation, Genie Code scheduled tasks at general availability, Anthropic Claude Fable 5.1 on Unity Gateway, Apache Arrow support for Zerobus Ingest at general availability, and automatic change data feed at general availability. Three deprecations land this month and are worth flagging to any joint customer: public S3 links for the legacy Simba JDBC Driver are being disabled, the Terraform deployment engine is being dropped from new Databricks CLI releases, and dedicated group clusters become the default for compliance-security-profile workspaces. Releases are staged, so accounts may not see the September features for a week or more. Further back, Data + AI Summit in June 2026 produced Genie One, Genie Ontology, Agent Bricks, Omnigent, Unity Catalog Metrics, Unity AI Gateway, Catalog Federation, Lakebase, LTAP and Lakehouse RT.

Vespa (Vespa.ai)

Quiet

No Vespa announcements in the window, though the nearest catalyst on the whole board is Vespa's and it starts tomorrow. Correcting the date carried in the previous edition: the main Vespa.ai Live event is 10 September at the Lumiere London Underwood Flagship Venue, with pre-event training on 9 September — "Vespa 101: Getting started with Vespa" and "Ranking 202: A deeper dive into improving retrieval quality" — alongside a joint evening with the London Information Retrieval & AI Meetup Group and the London Search & AI User Group on the same day. Speakers include Walmart, Etsy and RavenPack plus a keynote from co-founder and CEO Jon Bratseth, and the event is explicitly pitched at "teams exploring alternatives beyond traditional vector databases" — which is a positioning claim aimed as much at Elastic and Pinecone as at the pure vector vendors, and worth having someone monitor the talk abstracts. The strategically interesting standing item is the August 2026 partnership with Voyage AI by MongoDB, introducing an architecture that removes the recurring cost of regenerating embeddings for every user query — an unusual arrangement given MongoDB competes with Vespa directly, and one that reads as Vespa choosing embedding-cost economics over competitive tidiness. Earlier 2026 work centred on developer surface and operability: the Vespa Kubernetes Operator and the Vespa.ai Playground in February, Pyvespa 1.0 in May, a Nexla partnership announced 18 February 2026 covering real-time AI search across enterprise data sources, and .NET support for Vespa.ai OSS contributed by Outmatic.
