Vendor detail

Pinecone

Quiet

Nothing from Pinecone in the window, but the standing picture changed materially just before it and deserves flagging. On 2 September, full-text search and the new Documents API reached general availability on API version 2026-07: schema-based document indexes that combine full-text BM25, dense-vector and sparse-vector ranking fields in a single index, queried with BM25 token matching, Lucene query syntax or vector similarity and narrowed by metadata and text-match filters. It is a breaking change at the REST layer — on 2026-07, POST /indexes is schema-only and the top-level dimension, metric, vector_type and spec fields are replaced by a schema. Full-text search had been in public preview since 4 May 2026, with fuzzy matching and n-gram substring search added on 21 July. The read is that the pure-play vector vendor has decided it cannot win agentic retrieval on dense vectors alone, and is rebuilding toward the hybrid index Elasticsearch already is. On commercial terms, 1 September brought egress metering with monthly allowances — 1 GB on Starter, 10 GB on Builder, 100 GB on Standard and Enterprise, with reads blocked past the allowance on the two smaller plans — and the withdrawal of the $250 bulk import credit for new subscriptions. Bring Your Own Cloud reached general availability on 25 August across AWS, GCP and Azure, and Pinecone Nexus, the knowledge engine for agents, went GA on 6 August. Ash Ashutosh has been CEO since September 2025; that appointment is not new and search results still resurface it as current.

Weaviate

Quiet

No Weaviate announcements in the window. The most consequential recent item is Engram, Weaviate's managed memory and context service for agentic applications, which reached general availability in June 2026 — a bet that the durable moat in agent infrastructure is persistent memory rather than the vector index underneath it. On the core engine, v1.39.0 shipped in August 2026 with a new quantization method, new query surfaces, runtime schema control and performance work across search, replication and indexing, following the HFresh disk-based vector index introduced in June 2026; the pair address the memory-cost objection that has dogged Weaviate in large deployments. On distribution, Weaviate published Agent Skills as an open-source repository on 21 February 2026, equipping Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, VS Code and Gemini CLI with tools for generating production Weaviate code — placing Weaviate inside the coding agents where retrieval choices increasingly get made. Funding remains the structural weakness: roughly $67.6 million reported to date, against rivals carrying several times that.

OpenSearch (AWS / Linux Foundation)

Quiet

Nothing from the OpenSearch project or Amazon OpenSearch Service in the window, and the quiet is almost certainly deliberate: OpenSearchCon North America runs 22–24 September in San Jose, thirteen days out, marking the project's fifth anniversary with more than 60 keynotes, lightning talks, breakouts and workshops. The schedule, published 23 July 2026, lists speakers from Apple, AWS, CERN, IBM, Intel and LinkedIn alongside engineering sessions from Autodesk, Booking.com, Groupon, NetApp Instaclustr and Uber, framed around open-source search and observability powering agentic AI at enterprise scale. On product, the latest project release remains OpenSearch 3.8.0 from 5 August 2026, which added experimental retention policies for agentic memory that automatically expire sessions, long-term memories and history entries. The managed service has been shipping steadily: automatic semantic enrichment extended to VPC-enabled domains in August 2026, giving private-network customers context-aware retrieval without public exposure; an additional upgrade runway and support dates for older versions announced in August 2026; and in July 2026 both OpenSearch 3.7 support, with 1-bit scalar quantization on the Faiss and Lucene engines, and a purpose-built log-analytics engine claiming up to 4x better price-performance and up to 70% lower storage through columnar storage for aggregation workloads. Expect September's announcement backlog to clear at the conference.

Algolia

Quiet

No Algolia news in the window. The company's most recent substantive product move is the Agent Studio expansion of 28 July 2026, which added per-agent guardrails, cost controls and governance: custom disallowed-content categories applied to shopper inputs, agent outputs or both, with configurable fallback messages, plus global and per-IP request limits, maximum tokens per response, conversation-depth limits and approved-domain lists. It also brought AI mode into the search bar itself, so a shopper typing a query gets contextual prompt suggestions that open a conversational session pre-populated. Strategically, Algolia set out its position in "Six Bold Bets for 2026" on 13 January 2026, arguing that retrieval, scale and memory are the foundation of agentic AI — a retrieval-infrastructure framing that moves Algolia onto the ground Elastic occupies rather than the e-commerce search box where it has historically competed. DevCon 2026, a free virtual conference, runs 30 September to 1 October with an agenda built around AI agents rather than site search. Note that search engines still resurface Algolia's original Agent Studio launch and its DocSearch/AskAI release as recent; both are September 2025.

Qdrant

Quiet

Nothing new from Qdrant inside the window, following an unusually loud start to the month. On 1 September Qdrant released Qdrant-FineWeb-10B, built with Vultr on Hugging Face's FineWeb corpus using Alibaba's gte-multilingual-base: 10.07 billion dense and 10.07 billion sparse vectors, 24.47 TB of vectors and 28.66 TB of source text and metadata, with exact brute-force top-1000 ground truth computed for 120,000 dense, sparse and filtered queries. Alongside it came Supernova, an open-source benchmarking framework whose nova-load and nova-storm modules drive parallel ingestion and search stress tests against Qdrant, Milvus and Elasticsearch, tracking QPS, p50/p95/p99 latency, build time and recall against nova-bf ground truth — a reproducible harness in which Elasticsearch is a named target, framed as an attack on vendor benchmarks that use synthetic data, hidden queries and paywalled engines. Press pickup followed in Blocks & Files on 1 September and BigDATAwire on 3 September. On the engine, Qdrant 1.19.0 shipped on 4 August 2026 with TurboQuant 4-bit primary vector storage, a unified cold/cached/pinned memory strategy and a global quota API. The next catalyst is Vector Space Stream on 17 September, a research-first broadcast of eight technical talks on compression, edge and hybrid search. Funding stands at $87.5 million total after a $50 million Series B in March 2026.

MongoDB (Atlas Search / Atlas Vector Search)

Quiet

No MongoDB product news in the window, but the investor calendar is dense and starts today. MongoDB presents at the Citi 2026 Global TMT Conference on 9 September at 8:50 a.m. Eastern, the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference on 10 September at 10:10 a.m. Pacific, and the Piper Sandler Growth Frontiers Conference on 15 September at 11:30 a.m. Central — three chances in a week for management to restate the Atlas retrieval narrative to the buy side. The financial backdrop is the 1 September Q2 FY2027 print: revenue of $771.8 million, up 30% year over year and ahead of a roughly $734 million consensus, with Atlas up about 29%, a return to GAAP profitability at $40.9 million net income against a $47 million loss a year earlier, 70,600 customers after a record 2,900 net additions, and full-year guidance raised to $2.99–3.03 billion. On product, the relevant work is dated 13 August 2026: a Managed MCP Server connecting Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build and Devin directly to Atlas operational data, and an Atlas retrieval push comprising Automated Embeddings powered by Voyage AI, the Atlas Embedding and Reranking API, voyage-code-4, and vector search inside Atlas Stream Processing. Vector search over nested embeddings reached general availability on 30 June 2026.

Databricks

Active

Databricks posted three dated items inside the window. On 7 September, ingesting into tables backed by default storage with Zerobus Ingest in Lakeflow Connect entered public preview. On 8 September, ABAC DENY policies went to beta in Unity Catalog, letting an administrator explicitly deny the MANAGE ACCESS CONTROL privilege to named principals — including object owners — with the deny always taking precedence over any grant; the same day brought a time_bucket SQL function that returns the start of the fixed-width interval a timestamp falls into, aligned to a chosen origin, for bucket widths that are not calendar units.

None of it is retrieval. The search-relevant Databricks work this month is dated 2 September, when AI Search added indexing and filtering on struct and map columns plus filter-only queries that return rows matching a filter with no vector or keyword retrieval at all. Read across the two weeks, the pattern is a platform closing governance and ingestion gaps at a faster cadence than it closes retrieval gaps — which is consistent with a company that expects to win the search workload by owning the data plane rather than by out-ranking a dedicated engine. Note that releases are staged and accounts may not see these features for a week or more.

Watch signal: Deny-by-default access control in Unity Catalog removes one of the recurring reasons regulated customers keep a separate, separately-governed search tier; the counter-argument for Elastic has to rest on retrieval quality and operational maturity, not on access-control parity.

Standing items: Data + AI Summit in June 2026 produced Genie One, Genie Ontology, Agent Bricks, Omnigent, Unity Catalog Metrics, Unity AI Gateway, Catalog Federation, Lakebase, LTAP and Lakehouse RT, and Databricks reports more than 100,000 agents built on Agent Bricks. Earlier September items include OpenAI GPT-6 Astra on Unity Gateway for agentic search and long-context work (4 September), OpenSharing for metric views in beta and environment version 6 (3 September), Git Folder Serverless in beta and Google Gemini 3.8 Flash as a hosted model (2 September), and on 1 September Genie One web search in beta with source citation, Genie Code scheduled tasks at general availability, Anthropic Claude Fable 5.1 on Unity Gateway, Apache Arrow support for Zerobus Ingest at GA, and automatic change data feed at GA, which computes row-level changes at query time and makes MERGE and UPDATE roughly 15% faster on tables queried for changes.

Vespa (Vespa.ai)

Active

Vespa.ai Live is running now. The event opened today, 9 September, at Lumiere London with a full day of hands-on training — Vespa 101 for beginners and Ranking 202 on improving retrieval quality — followed this evening by the London Information Retrieval & AI Meetup, a joint session with the London Search & AI User Group. The main conference day is tomorrow, 10 September, opening with a keynote from co-founder and CEO Jon Bratseth on the architectural shifts behind commerce, search, recommendation and agentic applications.

The agenda is the story. Cem Ünsal, a Distinguished Engineer at Walmart, presents on taking Vespa to production across verticals; Gregg Donovan of Etsy covers Etsy's migration to a unified Vespa-based platform, including LLM-assisted retrieval migrations and distilling teacher models into fast GBDT rankers; Dainius Jocas of RavenPack asks whether the HNSW index can be eliminated entirely for high-volume financial data using binarized embeddings; and Christiane Lemke and André Charton describe Kleinanzeigen/Adevinta's move from a legacy relevance system to a Vespa-powered recommendation platform. Trey Grainger introduces "wormhole vectors" for jumping between sparse lexical, dense semantic and behavioural vector spaces at query time, Doug Turnbull reports a year of autoresearch on ranking, and Sease demonstrates open-source LLM tooling for search-quality evaluation. A closing panel puts Vinted and Searchplex alongside Bratseth. A livestream is open to remote registrants.

Watch signal: This is a competitor assembling, in one room and on the record, the named enterprise migration stories Elastic normally has to supply from its own case-study library — Walmart, Etsy, RavenPack, Vinted and Kleinanzeigen all describing why they moved and what they gained. Session content, particularly the RavenPack talk on removing HNSW and Grainger's cross-space retrieval, is worth reading in full when recordings post.

Standing items: Vespa's August 2026 partnership with Voyage AI by MongoDB introduces an architecture that removes the recurring cost of regenerating embeddings for every user query — an unusual arrangement given MongoDB competes with Vespa directly, and one that reads as Vespa choosing embedding-cost economics over competitive tidiness. Earlier 2026 work centred on developer surface and operability: the Vespa Kubernetes Operator and Vespa.ai Playground in February, Pyvespa 1.0 in May, a Nexla partnership announced 18 February 2026 covering real-time AI search across enterprise data sources, and .NET support for Vespa.ai OSS contributed by Outmatic.

Method, judgment calls and discarded items

  1. Window. The strict 72-hour window is 6–9 September 2026, measured to 07:29 UTC on 9 September. Two vendors had verifiable in-window developments: Databricks (three dated release-note items, 7–8 September) and Vespa (Vespa.ai Live opening today in London). The other six are marked no-update rather than stretching the window to fill the page.
  2. Vespa counted as active. Vespa published no release or press item in the window, but its flagship event began inside it with a verified published agenda. That is a real in-window development and is recorded as one; the card states explicitly what is happening today versus tomorrow.
  3. Coverage gap corrected. The previous edition covered 5–8 September and reported all eight vendors quiet. It missed Pinecone's 2 September general availability of full-text search and the Documents API, which is the most competitively significant item of the past ten days. It appears here as explicitly dated context, not as an in-window development.
  4. Discarded as misdated. Search results resurfaced each of these as current; all are wrong. Pinecone's appointment of Ash Ashutosh as CEO is 18 September 2025, not September 2026 — the TechTarget interview carries a 2026 site copyright that misleads. Algolia's Agent Studio expansion is 28 July 2026, not 4 September 2026 as one search snippet claimed; the original Agent Studio launch and DocSearch/AskAI are September 2025. Qdrant 1.19.0 is 4 August 2026, not September. There is no OpenSearch 3.9.0; 3.8.0 of 5 August 2026 remains current.
  5. Standing items refreshed. Qdrant funding corrected to $87.5 million total after the $50 million Series B of March 2026 (the previous edition said $50 million to date). Vespa.ai Live corrected from "London on 9 September" to 9–10 September, with 9 September being training and the meetup and 10 September the conference. MongoDB's investor calendar extended to all three September appearances. Weaviate's Engram GA (June 2026) added as the leading recent item.
  6. Template access. This edition was rebuilt rather than edited in place. The previous portal sits under ~/Library/Application Support, which the file tools block as a protected location, so the original CSS, JS and markup could not be read and were reconstructed to Elastic brand specification. Move the portal to a normal folder such as ~/Documents/search-compintel/ and repoint both the scheduled task and push_search.py so future runs can edit in place.
