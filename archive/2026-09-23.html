That release is still the one that matters: full-text search and the Documents API reached general availability on API version 2026-07, adding schema-based document indexes that combine BM25 full-text, dense-vector and sparse-vector ranking in one index. Standing items: Bring Your Own Cloud reached general availability on 25 August 2026 across AWS, GCP and Azure; Pinecone Nexus, the knowledge engine for agents, reached general availability on 6 August 2026; Pinecone Marketplace reached end of life on 31 August 2026; read-egress metering and the withdrawal of the one-time $250 bulk import credit for new Standard and Enterprise subscriptions both took effect 1 September 2026; and the last disclosed funding round remains the $100 million Series B of April 2023.

OpenSearch (AWS / Linux Foundation) Active

OpenSearchCon North America opened 22 September at the San Jose Marriott and runs through 24 September, organised by the Linux Foundation to mark the OpenSearch project's fifth anniversary. The conference frames open-source search, observability and analytics as the backbone for agentic AI at enterprise scale, with more than 60 keynotes, lightning talks, breakout sessions and hands-on workshops. Confirmed keynote speakers include Tia White of AWS on "The Next Five: Speed, Collaboration, and the Future We Build Together," Bill Pearson of Intel on co-designing search and analytics infrastructure, and Sokratis Papadopoulos of CERN on ten years of search at CERN, alongside real-world architecture talks from Booking.com, Groupon, LinkedIn and Uber.

The session to watch is Apple's Abhishek Singh on "Retrieval — The Hidden Backbone of Modern AI Systems." A hyperscale consumer technology company choosing to keynote on retrieval architecture, at an open-source search conference rather than at its own event, is a stronger signal of where enterprise AI infrastructure conversations are heading than any single vendor press release this window.

Watch signal: When AWS, Apple, Intel and CERN all keynote the same open-source search conference, retrieval infrastructure is being treated as foundational AI plumbing rather than a niche database category — which raises, not lowers, the bar for what Elastic's own retrieval story needs to demonstrate to hyperscale and research-heavy accounts.

Standing items: keynotes are being streamed via the OpenSearch Foundation's own channel for those not attending in person. Live attendees receive a $250 voucher toward the OpenSearch Certified Associate certification course, launching in the second half of 2026. The current stable release remains OpenSearch 3.8.0, shipped 5 August 2026; no OpenSearch 3.9 release could be verified as of this edition, so any version announced from the conference stage will be confirmed in the next edition rather than assumed here.

Databricks Active

Databricks logged six further items in its September platform release notes dated 21 and 22 September. On 21 September, the account-wide system.query.history system table reached general availability, giving admins query execution status, duration and source across SQL warehouses and serverless compute, and Databricks added support for backing a model provider service's API key with a customer-owned Unity Catalog secret, so credential rotation takes effect without a service change. On 22 September, three items shipped in Beta or general availability — a Shopify connector in Lakeflow Connect for ingesting e-commerce data, a Lakebase backup schedule API for programmatically managing snapshot cadence and retention, and two new OpenAI models, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, on Unity Gateway.

None of the six items touches retrieval or AI Search directly; they are governance, connectivity and model-catalogue work. The retrieval-relevant item remains the generate_citations option in ai_search, which entered beta on 8 September and was reported in an earlier edition.

Watch signal: Databricks continues to close operational and governance gaps around the lakehouse at a steady weekly cadence, which narrows the non-retrieval reasons an account might need a second system — leaving relevance and latency evidence, not feature checklists, as the argument Elastic has to keep making.

Standing items: the Genie One MCP server is scheduled to reach general availability on 25 September, after this edition's window closes; it will be confirmed as shipped in the next edition rather than reported early. Releases are staged, so accounts may not see any of the above features for a week or more after each release date.
